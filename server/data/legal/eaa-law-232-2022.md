# European Accessibility Act (Directive 2019/882) and Romania's Law 232/2022

> Informational summary, not legal advice. Last checked: 22 October 2026.
> Sources: [Directive (EU) 2019/882 (EUR-Lex)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj),
> [Legea 232/2022 on legislatie.just.ro](https://legislatie.just.ro/).

## What it is

The European Accessibility Act (EAA) sets common accessibility requirements for certain **products and services**
sold to consumers in the EU. Romania transposed it through **Law 232/2022**. The requirements apply from **28 June 2025**.

## What it covers (main examples)

- Products: computers and operating systems, smartphones, self-service terminals (ATMs, ticket machines), e-readers.
- Services: **e-commerce**, consumer **banking** services, electronic communications, e-books, websites and apps for passenger transport.

Microenterprises (fewer than 10 employees and up to €2 million turnover) that provide **services** are exempt.

## How accessibility is measured

Products and services that follow the harmonised standard **EN 301 549** (which includes WCAG 2.1 AA for web content) are presumed to comply.
WCAG 2.2 AA is the current best practice.

## What it means for Brightline

- The EAA is about products and services **for consumers**. Brightline sells B2B billing software, so the Act may not apply directly
  to its product. Internal workplace tools are covered by employment law (Directive 2000/78, Law 448/2006), not by the EAA.
- EN 301 549 and WCAG 2.2 are still the reference when the company buys or builds accessible internal tools (F9 "accessible tooling").

## How Bridge uses it

- Bridge itself targets WCAG 2.2 AA (keyboard-only use, screen readers, contrast, reflow at 320 px, reduced motion).
- F7 cites it for the "accessible tools" adjustment; F9 cites EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.2 as the reference standard.
