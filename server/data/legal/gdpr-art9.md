# GDPR Article 9: Health data is a special category

> Informational summary, not legal advice. Last checked: 22 October 2026.
> Source: [Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Article 9 (EUR-Lex)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

## What it says

- Data concerning **health** (which includes information about a disability) is a **special category** of personal data.
- Processing it is **prohibited**, unless one of the exceptions in Article 9(2) applies. The ones that matter at work:
  - **9(2)(b):** processing is necessary to carry out the employer's obligations in employment law, for example to provide reasonable accommodation;
  - **9(2)(a):** the person gives **explicit consent** (at work, consent is often not considered freely given, so employers usually rely on 9(2)(b)).
- The general principles still apply: collect only what is necessary (**data minimisation**, Article 5(1)(c)), keep it secure,
  and limit who can see it.
- Article 88 lets member states set more specific rules for personal data in the employment context.

## What it means in practice

- The **People team** may need to know about a condition to arrange an adjustment. Your **manager and team** usually only need to know
  **what changes** (for example "written standup updates", "captions in meetings"), not why.
- Health information should not be shared in public Slack channels or stored in tickets.
- You can ask what is stored about you and who can see it (right of access, Article 15).

## How Bridge follows it

- Bridge never stores a diagnosis. The profile stores display and output preferences only, and the free text you type is not saved.
- The F7 letter contains needs and proposed adjustments only.
- Anonymous questions are stored without a user id.
- F9 checks whether the company's adjustment policy has a confidentiality clause (Brightline's does not).
