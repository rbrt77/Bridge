# EU Directive 2000/78/EC, Article 5: Reasonable accommodation

> Informational summary, not legal advice. Last checked: 22 October 2026.
> Source: [Council Directive 2000/78/EC (EUR-Lex)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/78/oj).
> The Directive sets a general framework for equal treatment in employment and occupation in all EU member states, including Romania.

## What Article 5 says

To guarantee equal treatment for persons with disabilities, **employers must take appropriate measures, where needed in a particular case**,
to enable a person with a disability to:

- have access to employment,
- do the job,
- advance in their career, and
- take part in training,

**unless these measures would impose a disproportionate burden** on the employer.
The burden is not disproportionate when it is sufficiently covered by existing state measures (for example, public funding for equipment).

## Examples (Recital 20 of the Directive)

Effective and practical measures to adapt the workplace to the disability, for example:

- adapting premises and **equipment**,
- **patterns of working time**,
- the **distribution of tasks**,
- the provision of **training** or integration resources.

## Related rules

- Disability is one of the protected grounds (with religion or belief, age and sexual orientation). Direct and indirect discrimination are prohibited (Article 2).
- Member states must protect employees against retaliation for making a complaint (Article 11).

## What it means for a junior developer

- Written standups, captions, a different schedule, a screen reader setup or a different split of tasks are the kind of measures the Directive has in mind.
- The employer may refuse only if the measure is a disproportionate burden. Most developer adjustments cost little or nothing.
